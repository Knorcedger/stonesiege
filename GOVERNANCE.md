# StoneSiege governance

StoneSiege is maintained in public. Players and contributors should be able to understand how direction is set, challenge an idea respectfully, and build a fork when the official project chooses another path.

## Roles

- **Players** report problems, test releases, and take part in design discussions.
- **Contributors** submit code, art, writing, tests, research, or documentation.
- **Reviewers** are trusted contributors who help evaluate changes in an area.
- **Maintainers** merge releases, protect credentials and player safety, enforce community standards, and make final project decisions.

Roles are earned through sustained, constructive work and can be scoped to a subsystem. Maintainers may add or remove roles after a public explanation, except where privacy, safety, or security requires discretion.

## Decisions

Routine fixes and small improvements are decided through normal issue and pull-request review. For changes that materially affect game direction, compatibility, monetization, licensing, privacy, or architecture:

1. Open a proposal issue describing the player problem, options, tradeoffs, and migration impact.
2. Leave meaningful time for players and contributors to respond.
3. Summarize the strongest arguments and any playtest evidence.
4. A maintainer records the decision and rationale.

Consensus is preferred, but this is not government by poll. Maintainers make the final call and remain accountable for explaining it. Legal, security, privacy, store-policy, and urgent operational decisions may be made privately or quickly, with as much later disclosure as is safe.

## Player input and money

Official paying players may receive access to surveys or multiplayer-specific feedback channels because they use that product, but payment never buys votes or authority over unpaid players and contributors. Broad game-direction discussions should remain publicly accessible whenever practical.

The current principles are:

- The existing single-player game remains free.
- If official multiplayer is released commercially, the intended model is a one-time purchase.
- No ads, loot boxes, subscriptions, or pay-to-win mechanics.
- A business-model change requires a public proposal before implementation, except where immediate legal compliance is necessary.

These are project-direction commitments, not a promise that every planned feature or service will ship forever.

## Forks

The project's open licenses are an important check on governance. Anyone may build a technical fork under those licenses. Distributed forks must use a distinct identity as described in [TRADEMARK.md](TRADEMARK.md).

## Conduct and appeals

The [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) applies in project spaces. A person affected by a moderation or maintainer decision may request a second review by emailing [support@flarmio.com](mailto:support@flarmio.com). When the same maintainer is the only available reviewer, the project will document that limitation and seek an independent community perspective where practical.
