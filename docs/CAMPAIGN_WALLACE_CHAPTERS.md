# William Wallace campaign — chapter structure

The live campaign turns the original six long scenarios into twelve shorter save
boundaries. Economy and preparation chapters end before their paired battle, while
the decisive battles begin with an appropriate force already in the field.

## Historical framing

The chronology follows the documented spine: Lanark, Scone, Stirling Bridge, the
Northumberland campaign, Guardianship, Falkirk, Wallace’s diplomacy in France,
Happrew, the uncertain action at “Yrenside,” capture near Glasgow on 5 August 1305,
and execution at Smithfield on 23 August 1305. Briefing text distinguishes late
tradition from evidence and explicitly labels the final Earnside battle as a
dramatization because the surviving record preserves almost no tactical detail.

Battlefield layouts, exact troop counts, individual dialogue, resource economies,
and connective tactical objectives are game abstractions. The original six scenario
ids stay registered outside the visible campaign order so old saves continue to load;
campaign progress migrates each completed legacy scenario to its two replacement
chapters.

## Interlude art

Five 16:9, text-free historical paintings provide a consistent visual identity for
the five acts. They use period-plausible late-thirteenth-century mail, wool clothing,
weapons, and landscapes while deliberately avoiding cinematic inventions such as
kilts, blue face paint, and fantasy armor. Shipping WebP files total under 500 KB.
